The [Kiwifu/adhan-mp3 collection](https://github.com/Kiwifu/adhan-mp3) repository
About description states: **“Free for Islamic apps, prayer-time software, and
personal use.”** That statement was checked on 19 November 2026. It is a
repository-publisher statement, not a standard open-content license; neither a
Creative Commons license nor public-domain status is asserted. The collection
does not provide an individual rights-holder license for each named performer.
Attribution follows the collection's filenames and README; identity has not been
independently authenticated. The pinned source revision is
`b9180a2bb769f74cff8e378ef2cc7aaf8db5cd5b`. The About description is repository
metadata and is not part of that Git revision.

Exact filenames, source URLs, Git blob hashes and SHA-256 checksums are preserved
in `tools/athan_audio_manifest.json`. In-app attribution is in
`asset/AUDIO_CREDITS.txt`, exposed through Settings.

`tools/prepare_featured_audio.py` uses `imageio-ffmpeg==0.8.1` to verify original
checksums, decode each complete source, convert full recordings to mono 44.1 kHz
96 kbps MP3, and produce 28-second mono 22.05 kHz 16-bit PCM notification excerpts
with a one-second fade. No words are added or reordered. Android copies match
the full MP3 assets byte-for-byte. Conversion results, decoded durations and
output hashes are recorded in `tools/athan_audio_verification.json`.

## Athans added from their source, 20 November 2026

The recordings below are not in this repository and not in the app. The sound
picker lists them under **Trending athans**. When someone adds one, the phone
fetches it from the address recorded here, refuses it unless its size and
SHA-256 match `tools/athan_downloads.json`, and keeps it in the app's private
storage. Reminders play the stored file; nothing streams at prayer time.

They are fetched instead of bundled for two reasons. The muezzins asked for by
name, Muhammad Marwan Qassas, Muhammad Jazi Abdullah and Hashem Al-Saqqaf, have
no reuse licence stated at the source of their recordings, so the files are not copied
into a public repository. The collection entries would fit the app's
permission statement, but fifteen more recordings would take the APK past its
size limit.

The Hashem Al-Saqqaf recording (Dhuhr at the Grand Mosque, 1 Safar 1444) was
chosen as the cleanest complete one that could be reached: no clipping, a noise
floor near -62 dB, and nothing cut at either end. Others were rejected for a
cut-off last phrase (Isha, 26 Rabi' al-Awwal 1445), heavy clipping (Asr,
22 Muharram 1446), a host that no longer answers (Maghrib, 16 Dhu al-Qa'dah
1444) or a phone recording with its start missing. The chosen file was recorded
very quietly (-29 LUFS, against about -12 for the other athans), so after it
passes its check the phone raises it by 16.5 dB the way mp3gain does: it adds
eleven 1.5 dB steps to the global gain of every frame. The sound is scaled
exactly and nothing is re-encoded (`Mp3Cutter.louder`). Its highs stop near
11 kHz, as the source recording does.

The short Jazi athan is not a separate file. The phone cuts it from the full
recording at a frame boundary, in the pause before the first "Hayya
'ala-s-salah", without decoding or re-encoding (`lib/services/mp3_cutter.dart`).
The first frame after such a cut can refer to sound in the frame before it,
which players replace with 26 ms of silence; the cut sits in a pause so nothing
is lost.

"Trending" is the name of the group in the app, not a measured ranking. The
list holds the muezzins asked for, and muezzins and reciters whose athans
are widely shared and for which the collection holds a recording of 32 kb/s or
better. Recordings the collection only holds at 16 kb/s were left out.

| `qassas_clear.mp3`, `qassas_clear_short.wav` | Muhammad Marwan Qassas (محمد بن مروان), as identified by the app owner | [TikTok video by @chahr_2](https://www.tiktok.com/@chahr_2/video/7677998173700312353). No reuse licence is stated by the source. |

Unlike the fetched athans above, this recording is in the repository and the
APK. The app owner asked for it in the app, and its only source is a TikTok
post, which has no fixed download address for the phone to fetch and check.
It is listed in Trending athans as well, under the other Qassas recording:
its entry in `tools/athan_downloads.json` is marked `"bundled"`, so the phone
plays the copy in the app and fetches nothing.

The post's audio is an edit with added reverb, the "clear sound" it is shared
for. It is a different performance from the Qassas athan in Trending athans
(Asr, 3 Safar 1440): their pitch contours do not line up at any offset, speed
or key, while the same comparison still finds this recording after reverb, EQ
and a 2% speed change are added to it.

The source is the video as saved from the TikTok app: 21,495,839 bytes,
SHA-256 `2ae07feae82510440f44a56d87ff846e55c790fba4ebc141eedc037110bbba70`.
Its 128 kb/s AAC track is used up to 222.0 s; TikTok's end card and logo sound
follow and are left out. (The copy TikTok serves on the web carries the same
audio at 32 kb/s.) `tools/prepare_featured_audio.py --only qassas_clear
--source-dir DIR`, with that file in DIR as `qassas_clear.mp4`, rebuilds the
mono 44.1 kHz 96 kb/s MP3 and the 28-second iPhone excerpt with a one-second
fade, as for the featured recordings. There is no gain change: it measures
-16.7 LUFS, beside -17.1 for Nasser Al Qatami.
